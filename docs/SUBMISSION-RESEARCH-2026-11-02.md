## Recommended homepage and 2:58 proof strategy

These are design decisions inferred from the rubric and comparison set, not guarantees or attributed judge opinions.

1. **The first screen must make the product legible immediately:** original drawing/photo next to its recognizable playable world. One proposition, one creation action, one direct way to play a ready example. Avoid a dashboard of unexplained tools and long sales copy.
2. **Lead with authorship:** a person's marks and objects become the material of play. A human should recognize “I made that” without reading an architecture paragraph.
3. **A 6–10 second opening should show causality:** source artwork → that same art moving under keyboard control. Do not open with logos, model badges or narration over a static dashboard.
4. **One main story beats a tour:** use one visually legible source and one goal. Show real input, generation or a clearly labelled time cut, play, edit and replay. Keep the same source identity visible through the sequence.
5. **The second reveal is creator agency:** the user changes a real source/rule/object and the next play shows the specific consequence. Film only an edit supported by the actual product; a voiceover must not imply arbitrary code generation or unlimited mechanics.
6. **Variety is supporting proof:** briefly show a materially different drawing and a real photograph producing distinct interactions. Three different color skins on one platformer do not prove variety.
7. **Meta belongs inside the causal explanation:** identify only the models actually used in the recorded run, the task each performs, and why their output enables the experience. Hosted model provider is not interchangeable with model identity.
8. **Use the film to earn impact:** name the creative barrier removed, then demonstrate the independent task a person can complete. Do not invent user testimonials, retention, age-wide delight or market size.
9. **Keep evidence behind the frame:** repository README, model-run manifest, source/provenance record and honest timing can support the judge without filling everyday product UI with diagnostic text.
10. **End with a playable invitation:** a clear product name, concise promise, and a stable public link; give a viewer an immediate continuation after the film.

### Suggested 178-second allocation

| Time | Visible proof | What it earns |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:10 | Artwork-to-live-play match cut, real controls | Comprehension and surprise |
| 0:10–0:25 | One concrete creator problem and product promise | Impact |
| 0:25–1:05 | Actual input→generated world→playable objective | Working product and Meta contribution |
| 1:05–1:35 | Change a meaningful element, replay the changed world | Distinctive authorship and technical ambition |
| 1:35–1:55 | Contrasting drawing/photo, distinct interaction | Breadth without universal claims |
| 1:55–2:20 | Minimal visual explanation of actual model pipeline | Meta tooling and technical execution |
| 2:20–2:43 | Save/reopen, responsive play, compact verified evidence | Reliability and credibility |
| 2:43–2:58 | Strong final gameplay image and invitation | Memorable close |

A good film must remain understandable muted. Use concise captions, readable game scale, purposeful cursor motion, and clear separation of live speed from accelerated generation. Sound should support visible actions; music must not mask the explanation. Verify the final exported runtime, audio and public playback, rather than relying on the editor timeline.
